Overparameterized models routinely fit their training data exactly and still
generalize, which the classical bias-variance picture does not anticipate. We
study what the learned representation looks like in this regime. Writing the
predictor as a composition of an encoder $f_\theta$ and a readout $g_\phi$, we
show that the encoder concentrates on a low-dimensional subspace whose dimension
tracks the intrinsic dimension of the data rather than the parameter count. We
give a bound on the excess risk in terms of that effective dimension, and verify
on synthetic mixtures and standard image benchmarks that the predicted scaling
holds across three orders of magnitude in model size.
